\begin{abstract}
Vlog-based depression detection aims to identify depression-related behavioral patterns from naturalistic social-media videos. However, unconstrained vlog recordings contain large variations in facial visibility, speaking style, audio quality, and recording environment, making it difficult to capture structured facial-audio relations with sequential or global multimodal representations. This paper proposes an Action Unit-informed facial-audio graph network (AFGNN) for this task. Facial landmarks are represented as an AU-informed spatio-temporal graph, where facial-topology edges model local action-related relations and temporal edges capture landmark motion, and acoustic descriptors are represented as an audio temporal graph. The two graphs are encoded by modality-specific graph attention branches and integrated through bidirectional graph-level cross-modal gating. Under a five-seed protocol on D-Vlog, AFGNN achieves an F1-score of 0.788 and an AUC of 0.799, outperforming a strong Mamba-based baseline re-run under the identical official split. Retrained on LMVD with hyper-parameters carried over from D-Vlog, the framework maintains an AUC of 0.787, and a zero-shot face-only variant transfers across datasets without collapse. Ablation studies indicate the complementarity of the facial and audio branches and the benefit of graph-level gated fusion, and detect no statistically significant difference between the AU-informed static topology and learned dynamic graph alternatives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Depression is a common mental health disorder that affects emotional well-being, social functioning, and daily activities. Conventional screening relies on clinical interviews and self-report questionnaires, which are subject to reporting bias and unequal access to mental-health resources, motivating computational detection from observable behavior. Facial behavior and vocal prosody are widely used signals, as depression-related states may be reflected in reduced facial expressiveness, psychomotor changes, and altered speech~\cite{cohn2009detecting,cummins2015review,yang2013vocal}. While interview-based benchmarks such as AVEC and DAIC-WOZ provide controlled protocols for depression assessment~\cite{valstar2014avec,gratch2014daic}, user-generated vlogs capture behavior in less constrained social-media environments, and D-Vlog and LMVD have become representative vlog-based datasets~\cite{yoon2022dvlog,he2024lmvd}. The large variability of vlog recordings in facial visibility, speaking style, and recording conditions makes it difficult to rely on frame-level sequences or global multimodal features alone, and motivates structured behavioral representations.

Recent studies have improved temporal representation learning and multimodal fusion for depression detection, exploring time-aware attention, spatio-temporal transformers, Mamba-based sequence encoders, and cross-modal fusion~\cite{model:TAMFN,tao2024depmstat,xing2024emomamba,liu2024mddmamba,zhao2025decoupled}, and graph-based representations have been introduced to model spatio-temporal facial dependencies~\cite{xu2025twostage,model:MSI-Net}. However, facial landmarks are still mostly treated as coordinate sequences or generic spatio-temporal inputs, while their action-related organization remains insufficiently explored. Anatomically, landmarks are grouped around facial action-related regions described by the Facial Action Coding System (FACS), in which Action Units provide a standard description of visible facial muscle movements~\cite{ekman1978facs,baltrusaitis2018openface}, and graph-based relational reasoning has proven useful for modeling dependencies among facial regions and action units in expression and AU recognition~\cite{song2021uncertain,liao2022fergcn}. Since vlog-based datasets rarely provide AU annotations, recent work uses statistically significant Action Units as covariates to guide graph attention~\cite{zhao2026covariate}; we instead adopt an AU-informed design that encodes action-related topology structurally, without requiring AU labels.

We propose AFGNN, an Action Unit-informed Facial-Audio Graph Network for vlog-based depression detection. The framework organizes facial landmarks into an AU-informed spatio-temporal graph, where intra-frame topology edges connect landmarks within facial action-related regions and inter-frame temporal edges capture landmark motion, and represents acoustic descriptors as an audio temporal graph. The two graphs are encoded by lightweight graph attention branches and integrated through bidirectional graph-level cross-modal gating~\cite{li2025avsgnn}, yielding a structured representation that combines facial action-related topology, landmark motion, acoustic continuity, and cross-modal interaction.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item This study formulates vlog-based depression detection as an Action Unit-informed facial-audio graph learning problem, emphasizing structured modeling of landmark-level facial action organization.

\item The proposed framework constructs an AU-informed spatio-temporal facial graph and an audio temporal graph, and integrates them through bidirectional graph-level cross-modal gating.

\item Under a five-seed protocol on D-Vlog, AFGNN achieves an F1-score of 0.788 and an AUC of 0.799, outperforming a strong Mamba-based baseline re-implemented under the identical official split; cross-dataset experiments on LMVD and ablation studies further examine generalizability and design choices.
\end{itemize}


\section{AU-Informed Facial-Audio Graph Network}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overall Framework}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}, each vlog sample is represented by a facial landmark sequence \(P \in \mathbb{R}^{T_v \times 68 \times 2}\) and an acoustic descriptor sequence \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{T_a \times 25}\), and the goal is to predict a binary depression label \(y \in \{0,1\}\). The framework maps the two sequences into an AU-informed spatio-temporal facial graph \(G_f = (V_f, E_f, X_f)\) and an audio temporal graph \(G_a = (V_a, E_a, X_a)\), where \(V_m\), \(E_m\), and \(X_m\) denote the nodes, edges, and node features of modality \(m \in \{f,a\}\). After modality-specific graph attention encoding and graph-level cross-modal gating, the prediction is \(\hat{y} = \sigma(\mathrm{MLP}([\tilde{h}_f; \tilde{h}_a]))\), where \(\tilde{h}_f\) and \(\tilde{h}_a\) denote the gated facial and audio graph-level representations.

\subsection{Facial and Audio Graph Construction}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.58\textwidth]{Face graph.png}
\caption{AU-informed facial graph construction. (a) The 68-point landmark topology overlaid on the face, colored by facial action-related region. (b) Temporal edges connect the same landmark across consecutive frames.}
\label{fig:face_graph}
\end{figure*}

Each facial node \(v^f_{i,t} \in V_f\) represents landmark \(i\) at visual frame \(t\), with a feature vector concatenating landmark position, frame-wise displacement, and facial-region identity
\begin{equation}
x^f_{i,t} = [p_{i,t}; \Delta p_{i,t}; r_i], \quad
\Delta p_{i,t} =
\begin{cases}
p_{i,t} - p_{i,t-1}, & t > 1, \\
0, & t = 1,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where \(p_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{2}\) denotes the landmark coordinate and \(r_i \in \{0,1\}^{9}\) the facial-region identity, giving \(x^f_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{13}\). The nine regions cover the face contour, eyebrows, nose bridge and bottom, eyes, and outer and inner mouth. Thus, \(|V_f| = 68T_v\) and \(X_f \in \mathbb{R}^{68T_v \times 13}\).

The facial edge set \(E_f = E_{\mathrm{top}} \cup E_{\mathrm{temp}} \cup E_{\mathrm{self}}\) consists of intra-frame topology edges, inter-frame temporal edges, and self-loops. The topology edges \(E_{\mathrm{top}}\) are derived from the standard 68-point landmark layout and connect adjacent landmarks within facial action-related regions, using chain connections for open components and ring connections for closed components, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:face_graph}. The temporal edges \(E_{\mathrm{temp}}\) connect each landmark to itself across consecutive frames, and the self-loops \(E_{\mathrm{self}}\) preserve node-specific information during message passing. This construction organizes landmark motion according to facial action-related topology without requiring AU annotations.

Each audio node \(v^a_t \in V_a\) corresponds to one acoustic frame, whose feature is the standardized descriptor \(x^a_t = (a_t - \mu)/(\sigma + \epsilon)\), where \(\mu,\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{25}\) are training-set statistics applied to all splits. The audio edges form a bidirectional temporal chain between adjacent frames, modeling short-term acoustic continuity.

\subsection{Graph Encoding and Gated Fusion}

The facial and audio graphs are encoded by modality-specific graph attention branches, \(\mathbf{H}_m = \mathrm{GAT}_m(G_m)\), producing node representations \(h^m_u \in \mathbb{R}^{d_m}\) for each node \(u \in V_m\). A gated readout then aggregates the node representations into a graph-level embedding,
\begin{equation}
\beta^m_u =
\frac{\exp(g_m(h^m_u))}
{\sum_{v \in V_m}\exp(g_m(h^m_v))},
\quad
h_m = \sum_{u \in V_m} \beta^m_u h^m_u ,
\end{equation}
where \(g_m\) is a modality-specific gate network producing an unnormalized score per node, \(\beta^m_u\) is the normalized readout weight, and \(h_m\) is the graph-level embedding of modality \(m\).

Since each modality is summarized into a single graph-level embedding, the framework adopts graph-level gating rather than token-level cross-attention. With \(q_m = W^m_q h_m\) and \(k_m = W^m_k h_m\), which share a common projection dimension \(d_g\), the directional gates and residual updates are
\begin{equation}
g_{f \leftarrow a}
=
\sigma\left(\frac{q_f^\top k_a}{\sqrt{d_g}}\right),
\quad
\tilde{h}_f
=
h_f + g_{f \leftarrow a} W^{a \rightarrow f}_v h_a,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
g_{a \leftarrow f}
=
\sigma\left(\frac{q_a^\top k_f}{\sqrt{d_g}}\right),
\quad
\tilde{h}_a
=
h_a + g_{a \leftarrow f} W^{f \rightarrow a}_v h_f ,
\end{equation}
where \(W^{a \rightarrow f}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_f \times d_a}\) and \(W^{f \rightarrow a}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{d_a \times d_f}\) project one modality's graph-level embedding into the other's hidden feature space, and \(\sigma\) denotes the sigmoid function. In plain terms, \(g_{f \leftarrow a} \in (0,1)\) is a scalar gate controlling how much audio information is injected into the facial representation, and \(g_{a \leftarrow f}\) plays the symmetric role.

The model is optimized with focal loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{focal}} = -\alpha_t(1-p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)\), where \(p_t\) is the predicted probability of the ground-truth class and \(\alpha_t = \alpha y + (1-\alpha)(1-y)\); we set \(\alpha=0.25\) and \(\gamma=2\). At inference, \(K\) independently trained models are averaged at the probability level. We set \(K=5\), corresponding to the full seed set of our stability protocol rather than a tuned hyper-parameter, and select the decision threshold of the averaged probability on the validation set by F1-score.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Experiments are conducted on the public D-Vlog dataset~\cite{yoon2022dvlog}, which contains 961 vlogs from 816 individuals (555 depressed / 406 control). Following the official split, the dataset is divided into 647 training (375 / 272), 102 validation (57 / 45), and 212 test (123 / 89) samples with a ratio of 7:1:2; we verified that the official split is subject-disjoint (no channel identity spans two splits). Each sample provides 68 facial landmarks and 25-dimensional openSMILE descriptors; samples with failed face detection are retained with zero-filled landmark features rather than discarded, so that the audio branch remains usable, and no raw facial pixels or identifiable facial images are processed. Performance is evaluated using Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Area Under the ROC Curve; the held-out test set is used only for final evaluation, while the validation set is used for checkpoint selection and threshold calibration.

The framework is implemented with PyTorch Geometric~\cite{fey2019fast}. The facial graph uses \(T_v=32\) sampled visual frames and the audio graph \(T_a=32\) acoustic frames. The facial GAT branch contains three layers with hidden dimension 128 and four attention heads, the audio GAT branch two layers with hidden dimension 64 and four heads, and dropout is 0.3; the model contains approximately \(1.2 \times 10^5\) trainable parameters. The main configuration is trained with AdamW (learning rate \(10^{-3}\), weight decay \(10^{-3}\)), cosine-annealing scheduling, and focal loss, for up to 100 epochs with early stopping on validation AUC (patience 15) and gradient clipping at norm 1.0. Unless otherwise specified, all results are reported over five independently trained models with seeds 42--46 (mean $\pm$ standard deviation), ablation results use single models, and the final test result averages the five models at the probability level. Two earlier training recipes appear only in clearly marked ablation rows: the component-ablation family (Table~\ref{tab:face_ablation}) uses Adam (weight decay \(10^{-4}\)) with ReduceLROnPlateau scheduling and focal loss, and the rows marked \(^\ddagger\) in Table~\ref{tab:fusion_design} additionally replace focal loss with weighted BCE. The component ablations are compared against their own within-recipe full model, whereas the \(^\ddagger\) rows differ from the main configuration in the training recipe as well and therefore serve as descriptive comparisons only.

\subsection{Comparison with Existing Methods}

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} compares the proposed method with existing methods reported on D-Vlog. The proposed method achieves the highest F1-score (0.789 single model, 0.788 ensemble) and the highest recall (0.878) among the listed methods, and also yields the best AUC (0.799) among methods with reported or reproduced AUC values. High recall is particularly desirable for depression screening, where a missed case is typically more costly than a false alarm, and the moderate precision reflects this screening-oriented trade-off.

These cited figures are obtained under heterogeneous evaluation protocols (some methods use five-fold cross-validation, whereas this study follows the official split) and serve as reference points rather than strictly controlled comparisons. For a controlled comparison, we additionally re-ran DepMamba~\cite{model:DepMamba}, the strongest publicly available audio-visual baseline, using its official implementation and hyper-parameters under the identical official split (the DepMamba$^\dagger$ rows of Table~\ref{tab:comparison}). Under this protocol, AFGNN outperforms the re-run DepMamba by a clear margin in both its single-model and ensemble forms, and a single AFGNN model already surpasses the five-seed DepMamba ensemble.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Evaluation on LMVD}

To examine generalizability beyond D-Vlog, we train and evaluate AFGNN on LMVD~\cite{he2024lmvd} (1{,}823 vlogs; 908 depressed / 915 control) under a stratified 70/10/20 video-level split (1{,}276/182/365 samples; LMVD releases no subject identities, so video-level splitting is used), carrying over the entire model configuration and all hyper-parameters from D-Vlog and adapting only the audio input dimension to LMVD's 128-dimensional VGGish descriptors. For reference, MDDformer~\cite{he2024lmvd} reports a weighted F1 of 0.7685 on LMVD under ten-fold cross-validation, which is not directly comparable to our held-out split.

Table~\ref{tab:lmvd} reports the results. Without any dataset-specific tuning, AFGNN reaches an AUC of 0.787$\pm$0.004 on LMVD, matching its D-Vlog performance (0.788$\pm$0.009) with even lower seed variance. Separately, we test zero-shot transfer of the D-Vlog-trained \emph{face-only} model to the LMVD test set with its D-Vlog-calibrated threshold: it retains an AUC of 0.602 (vs.\ 0.640 in-domain) and a recall of 0.906, suggesting that the learned facial-graph cues transfer across domains without collapse, although in-domain training is clearly beneficial.

\subsection{Robustness and Subgroup Analysis}

We probe robustness at inference time using the five full-model checkpoints. To simulate face-detection failure, the facial features of a random fraction of frames are zeroed: masking 10\% or 25\% of frames barely affects performance (AUC 0.784 and 0.779 vs.\ 0.788 clean), degradation remains graceful at 50\% (AUC 0.740), and even with all facial input removed the audio branch alone sustains an F1-score of 0.728. Adding Gaussian noise at the per-sample acoustic standard deviation reduces the AUC by only 0.012. We further report subgroup point estimates (female $n=146$: F1 0.775 / AUC 0.788; male $n=66$: F1 0.814 / AUC 0.795) without claiming significance at this sample size, and aggregating probabilities over the 179 test subjects yields consistent subject-level results (F1 0.774 / AUC 0.797).

\textit{Graph structure.}
Table~\ref{tab:face_ablation} removes one facial-graph component at a time; the dynamic variants instead add per-frame \(k\)-nearest-neighbor edges connecting each landmark to its \(k\) most similar landmarks by cosine similarity of node features (\(k{=}1\)) or motion vectors (\(k{=}2\)). Relative to the within-recipe full model, removing any single component changes the mean F1-score by at most 0.019 (self-loops) and the mean AUC by at most 0.007, and paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests detect no significant removal at the 0.05 level (the minimum attainable two-sided $p$ with five paired seeds is 0.0625). We therefore do not claim large gains from individual components at this dataset scale: the contribution of the AU-informed graph lies in its structured and privacy-friendly inductive bias rather than in any single edge type. Across five seeds, no statistically significant difference was detected between the static AU-informed topology and the tested dynamic variants, and we do not claim equivalence given the limited number of seeds.

\textit{Modality and gated fusion.}
In Table~\ref{tab:fusion_design}, the audio-only model outperforms the face-only model (F1 0.748 vs.\ 0.712, both trained with the same recipe), indicating stronger independent acoustic cues in vlogs, while the face-only model is no longer degenerate (AUC 0.640). Within the main recipe, replacing graph-level gated fusion with simple concatenation lowers the F1-score from 0.789 to 0.780 and the AUC from 0.788 to 0.774, supporting the benefit of gating. Removing either modality degrades performance relative to the full model, although both variants use the earlier recipe, so the comparison is descriptive only.

\textit{Audio length, loss, and ensemble.}
The 32-frame audio setting outperforms both the 16-frame and 64-frame settings, indicating that a moderate acoustic context is more suitable for this task. The weighted-BCE variant, trained under the earlier recipe, underperforms the focal-loss main configuration. Probability averaging over the five seeds improves the AUC from 0.788 (single-model mean) to 0.799 at an essentially unchanged F1-score (0.788 vs.\ 0.789), with the ensemble size fixed to the full seed set rather than tuned on the test set.

\textit{Facial-region sensitivity.}
Masking each facial region's identity encoding and retraining under the five-seed protocol changes the mean F1-score by at most 1.5\% (inner mouth), and every drop is smaller than the corresponding seed-level standard deviation; paired Wilcoxon tests detect no significant difference from the full model for any region. The asymmetric left-eye/right-eye sensitivity observed in our preliminary single-seed experiments did not replicate across seeds. These results indicate that the AU-informed graph does not rely on any single facial region.

\textit{Limitations.}
The current design summarizes each modality into a single graph-level embedding before fusion, which may discard fine-grained temporal correspondences between facial motion and speech, and the fixed temporal chains encode only adjacent-frame relations; frame-level cross-modal alignment is a promising future direction. In addition, both datasets are of moderate scale, and with five seeds the minimum attainable Wilcoxon $p$-value is 0.0625, so component-level effects below seed-level variance cannot be resolved.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented AFGNN, an AU-informed facial-audio graph network for vlog-based depression detection that organizes facial landmarks and acoustic descriptors into structured graph representations integrated through graph-level gated fusion. Evaluated under a five-seed protocol on D-Vlog, AFGNN achieves an F1-score of 0.788 and an AUC of 0.799, and outperforms a strong Mamba-based baseline re-run under the identical official split. Retrained on LMVD with hyper-parameters carried over from D-Vlog, the framework maintains its performance, and it remains robust to missing facial input and acoustic noise. Future work will explore frame-level cross-modal alignment for fine-grained audio-visual correspondences and larger-scale evaluation.
